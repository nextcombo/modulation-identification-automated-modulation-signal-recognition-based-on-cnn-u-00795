# [Modulation Identification] Automated Modulation Signal Recognition Based on CNN under Phase Defects [Attached Code]

Introduction: Wireless communication networks are designed to efficiently manage spectrum resources to support wireless communications. One technology anticipated in spectrum management is Automatic Modulation Classification (AMC), a complex signal processing technique that operates at the physical layer. Specifically, AMC's primary function is to automatically identify the modulation schemes used in wireless transmissions and is expected to play a key role in effective spectrum utilization, dynamic spectrum allocation, and reducing overhead through the omission of pilot symbols [1] [2]. However, to provide reliable AMC services, it is necessary to identify and address all factors that may degrade its performance. In this regard, AMC, like any wireless communication-based application, faces various deficiencies, including phenomena such as fading and frequency offsets that adversely affect the transmitted symbols on the receiver side. Despite their widely recognized and addressed impact on symbol amplitude, these phenomena often go unconsidered when it comes to their effect on phase [3]. Particularly, phase defects (PI) only manifest as time shifts in the received signal, making them more challenging to detect and resolve. Local oscillator (LO) inaccuracies lead to inaccurate frequency and phase synchronization, further complicating detection, while a lack of accurate channel state information (CSI) exacerbates the negative impact of system-wide PI. Therefore, considering the significant challenges posed by PI, improvements to the AMC architecture are crucial for ensuring robust and reliable performance under PI conditions.
In recent years, various AMC (Adaptive Multi-Carrier) techniques have been studied to improve the classification accuracy under additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) and fading conditions. These techniques mainly focus on higher-order moment methods for feature extraction or convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for image recognition methods, aiming to correctly classify the constellation of received signals [2]. Regarding image recognition technology, AMC has shown potential as a physical layer deep learning application, with many CNN-based architectures explored due to their proficiency in recognizing spatial patterns in received data [4]. However, there is a critical need for achieving high precision and low time complexity in CNNs, especially in real-world scenarios where the computational cost due to the number of convolutional layers and their characteristics (e.g., filter and kernel dimensions) becomes an issue [5]. Moreover, even though CNN-based AMC has demonstrated high accuracy in modulation classification in the presence of AWGN, the adaptability of CNN architectures to mitigate the impact of PI during AMC processes has not been explored [6] [7]. Specifically, even when considering fading, as in [6], the phase impact on received symbols is ignored, making the framework unsuitable for PI scenarios. Finally, although [7]'s authors mentioned that the presence of PI significantly reduces classification accuracy, they did not adjust their framework to compensate for the PI effect. Therefore, according to the authors, there is currently no research addressing the appropriate modifications to CNNs to meet real-time requirements for AMC applications while enhancing AMC performance under PI conditions.
In this letter, we propose a novel low-complexity method suitable for the AMC framework based on CNNs, which operates in the presence of PI without increasing additional time complexity. Specifically, our approach involves the integration of polar coordinate transformations for receiving signals and the use of asymmetric kernels in convolutional layers to capture feature modulation patterns in the polar plane. To demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, we apply it to LeNet (LN) and AlexNet (AN), mature CNN architectures within AMC, achieving significant improvements in classification accuracy compared to traditional IQ mapping. This is particularly evident when polar coordinate transformations are combined with asymmetric kernels. More specifically, our results show that our method can improve classification accuracy even under conditions of low signal-to-noise ratio and severe PI, without increasing the time complexity of the CNN architecture. Moreover, when our method is applied to LN, its classification accuracy surpasses that of traditional AN, challenging the assumption that more complex CNN architectures inherently lead to better performance. Therefore, our method not only enhances the classification accuracy of CNN-based AMC under PI conditions but also paves the way for existing CNN-based AMC solutions to be more robust against PI without increasing their time complexity.
Conclusion In this letter, we address the challenges of AMC under PI conditions by focusing on maintaining low time complexity while enhancing AMC accuracy. Specifically, we propose a scheme that applies polar coordinate transformations and non-symmetric kernel dimensionality methods to every CNN-based AMC framework. The provided simulation results validate the PI resilience of the proposed AMC framework, demonstrating the gains in classification accuracy with increasing PI effects. Interestingly, our findings suggest that the proposed method can enable simpler CNN architectures (such as LN) to achieve better accuracy than more complex ones (such as AN), thereby challenging the prevailing notion that more complex architectures inherently lead to better performance. This highlights the importance of our approach in AMC applications, as it can improve classification accuracy in PI scenarios without increasing system complexity.
